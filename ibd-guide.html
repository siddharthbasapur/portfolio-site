A simple guide for life after an IBD diagnosis

IBD Life Starter is a resource page for people recently diagnosed with Crohn’s disease or ulcerative colitis. It organizes trusted medical information and real patient experience themes into simple lifestyle guides.

Important note: This page is for education and support only. It is not medical advice. Always talk to a gastroenterologist, doctor, or registered dietitian before making medical, diet, or medication decisions.

Start Here

What is IBD?

Inflammatory bowel disease, or IBD, is a term that mainly includes Crohn’s disease and ulcerative colitis. A new diagnosis can feel overwhelming, so the first step is learning the basics and building a care team.

Trusted medical information

First questions to ask

Newly diagnosed patients may want to ask their doctor what type of IBD they have, what treatment options exist, what symptoms to watch for, and when to seek urgent care.

Doctor visit guide

Adjustment takes time

Many people need time to adjust emotionally and practically after an IBD diagnosis. Learning about food, symptoms, stress, and daily routines can make the condition feel less confusing.

Lifestyle support

Food & Eating

Food triggers are personal

There is no single IBD diet that works for everyone. Some people notice that certain foods affect their symptoms, especially during flares. Tracking meals and symptoms can help identify patterns to discuss with a doctor or dietitian.

Trusted medical information

Eating during flares

During symptom flare-ups, some people may need temporary food changes. The right approach depends on the person, their symptoms, and their nutrition needs.

Needs medical guidance

Travel & Daily Life

Planning can reduce stress

Traveling with IBD can feel easier when people plan ahead. Common strategies include packing extra medication, carrying emergency supplies, and checking bathroom access before long trips.

Lifestyle guide

Patient experience theme

Many people with IBD say that carrying an emergency kit helps them feel more comfortable away from home. Common items include wipes, extra underwear, hand sanitizer, and plastic bags.
